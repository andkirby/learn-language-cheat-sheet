Cases

Question → role → form.

4 Fälle

Wo? / Wohin?

A1trigger → case

Wo? — where? (position, like English in the house) → usually Dativ with Wechselpräpositionen.

Wohin? — where to? (direction, like English into the house) → usually Akkusativ.

Ich bin in der Schule (Wo?).
Ich gehe in die Schule (Wohin?).
I am at school (where?).
I am going to school (where to?).

The quick meaning

A1usage

Akk. mostly = the direct object: I see — whom?

Dat. mostly = the receiver: I give — to whom?

Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch.
I give the man the book. dem Mann — receiver (Dat.); das Buch — direct object (Akk.).

Articles and pronouns

The main forms that actually change.

Formen

Definite article — der / die / das

A1forms
Mask.Neut.Fem.Plural
Nom.derdasdiedie
Akk.dendasdiedie
Dat.demdemderden
Gen.desdesderder
der / die / das — the (gender/case forms)
Der Mann kommt.
Ich sehe den Mann · mit dem Mann
The man is coming.
I see the man · with the man. der/den/dem all mean “the”; case changes the form.

English “the” never changes shape — German’s does: the ending is the case marker in action. Bold = the traps of this level: masculine Akkusativ and the whole Dativ column. Genitiv changes too, but A1–B1 needs it less.

Gender is not logic — die Brücke is not “she”: learn every noun with its article. Genitiv and Dativ plural also change the noun (des Mannes · den Kindern) — see Endings and changes.

Mask. — masculine; Neut. — neuter; Fem. — feminine; Plural — more than one.

dieser / diese / dieses

A1forms

Demonstrative: dieser/diese/dieses — this; it can point to “that” in context. Learn the forms with the der/die/das endings; distance is also expressed with hier/dort or jener.

dieser Mann · diese Frau · dieses Kind
Ich nehme diesen Bus. · Das ist schön.
This man · this woman · this child.
I’m taking this bus. · That’s nice.

der, ein or kein?

A1usage

der — known, already mentioned · ein — one of many, first mention · kein — negation (“no”).

Lehrer / Ärztin — teacher / doctor (female)
Das ist ein Hund. Der Hund ist groß. · Das ist kein Hund.
That is a dog. The dog is big. · That is not a dog.
ein introduces one; der refers to the known one; kein negates.

Professions normally omit the article when simply naming the job: Ich bin Lehrer — I’m a teacher; Sie ist Ärztin — She’s a doctor. An article can appear with emphasis or qualification: ein guter Lehrer — a good teacher.

ein / kein / mein…

A1forms

English possessives never change (my father, with my father) — German adds the ein-word endings: mein Vater → meinen Vater.

Nom.meinmeinmeinemeine
Akk.meinenmeinmeinemeine
Dat.meinemmeinemmeinermeinen
Gen.meinesmeinesmeinermeiner
ein — a/an (one) kein — no / not a mein — my dein — your (one familiar person) sein — his / its ihr — her / their unser — our euer — your (several familiar people) Ihr — your (polite)
Ich habe einen Bruder und eine Schwester.
mit meinem Bruder · mit meiner Schwester
I have a brother and a sister.
With my brother · with my sister.

Bold = the traps of this level: masculine Akkusativ and the whole Dativ column. Genitiv changes too, but A1–B1 needs it less.

* ein has no plural. The mein column is the model for all of them: kein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer take these endings on the same stem.

Mask. — masculine; Neut. — neuter; Fem. — feminine; Plural — more than one.

nicht or kein?

A1usage

kein = English “no” + noun — it replaces ein: kein Geld, keine Zeit.

nicht = “not” — everything else (verbs, adjectives, pronouns): the all-purpose negation.

Ich habe kein Auto. · Ich schlafe nicht.
I don’t have a car. · I’m not sleeping. kein — no/not a; nicht — not.

German has no do-support: the verb itself is negated — never rebuild “don’t” with a helper verb.

Prepositions

The preposition often decides the case outright.

Präpositionen

Always Akkusativ

A1trigger → case
durch — through für — for gegen — against / around (time) ohne — without um — around / at (time)
für den Mann · ohne mich
For the man · without me. The preposition fixes the case.

Always Dativ

A1trigger → case
aus — out of / from bei — at / near / with mit — with / by (transport) nach — after / to (city, country without article) seit — since / for (continuing duration) von — from / about / of zu — to
mit meinem Bruder · zu der Arbeit → zur Arbeit
With my brother · to work (zu der → zur). The preposition fixes the case.

Two cases: Wo? / Wohin?

A2trigger → case
an — at / on (contact) auf — on / onto hinter — behind in — in / into neben — next to über — above / over unter — under / among vor — in front of zwischen — between
auf dem Tisch (Wo?)
auf den Tisch (Wohin?)
On the table (where?) · onto the table (where to?). dem/den show the case.

Often Genitiv

B1trigger → case
wegen — because of trotz — despite während — during statt — instead of innerhalb — inside / within außerhalb — outside
wegen des Wetters
Because of the weather. des Wetters — Genitiv of das Wetter (weather).

In speech some of these also take Dativ, but the norm to learn is Genitiv.

Verb + preposition

A2trigger → case

The preposition almost never matches English: wait for → warten auf, think about → denken an.

Ich warte auf den Bus. · Er denkt an die Prüfung.
I’m waiting for the bus. · He’s thinking about the exam.
Learn the case with the whole pair: warten auf + Akk.; denken an + Akk.
warten auf + Akk. — wait for denken an + Akk. — think about sich freuen auf + Akk. — look forward to sich freuen über + Akk. — be pleased about sich interessieren für + Akk. — be interested in teilnehmen an + Dat. — take part in

seit or vor?

A2usage

seit + Dativ — since/for: Ich wohne seit einem Jahr hier — I have lived here for a year.

vor + Dativ — ago: Ich kam vor einem Jahr — I arrived a year ago.

Ich wohne seit zwei Jahren hier. · Ich bin vor zwei Jahren umgezogen.
I have lived here for two years. · I moved two years ago.
seit — continuing duration; vor — ago.

For a state continuing now, normally use Präsens: Ich wohne seit zwei Jahren hier — I have lived here for two years. seit itself does not rule out Perfekt.

Word order

Find the finite verb first.

Word order

Statements: verb in slot 2 (V2)

A1position
Position 1→verb→everything else
Ich gehe heute in den Laden.
Heute gehe ich in den Laden.
I’m going to the shop today.
Today I’m going to the shop. gehe — am going; the German finite verb stays in slot 2.

If the sentence starts with Heute / Dann / Deshalb…

A2position

…the fronted element takes slot 1 and the subject moves behind the verb.

Heute muss ich arbeiten.
Not: Heute ich muss…
Today I have to work. müssen → muss — have to; arbeiten — work.
After Heute the verb comes before ich.

English keeps “Today I must work” together; German keeps the verb in slot 2 and lets the subject fall behind.

Questions

A1position
Kommstdu heute?
Wannkommstdu?

Kommst du heute? — Are you coming today? · Wann kommst du? — When are you coming?

Yes/no → verb first: Sprichst du Deutsch? — there is no do-support; no helper verb is inserted.

W-question (wer, was, wann, warum, wie…) → question word + verb — exactly the English pattern.

wer / was — who / what wann / warum — when / why wie / wo / wohin — how / where / where to

TeKaMoLo

A2position

Temporal (when) → Kausal (why) → Modal (how) → Lokal (where)

Ich fahre heute wegen der Arbeit mit dem Zug nach Berlin.
I’m travelling to Berlin by train today for work.
heute — today; wegen der Arbeit — for work; mit dem Zug — by train; nach Berlin — to Berlin.

A neutral German middle-field order is time–cause–manner–place. English often places time at the end; neither language has one obligatory adverb order.

A safe default, not a law: emphasis and context can promote time or cause to slot 1.

Particles: doch, mal, denn…

B1usage

No literal translation — each one carries tone. English does this with intonation and small adverbs; learn the pairs:

doch — actually / on the contrary mal — just (a nudge) denn — then / so (in questions) ja — after all / you know schon — I suppose gar — at all (with nicht) wohl — probably
Komm mal her! · Das ist ja klar! · Wie heißt du denn?
Come here a moment! · That’s obvious, after all! · So, what’s your name?
These are contextual tone clues, not fixed translations.

Imperativ: commands & requests

A1forms

Use the imperative for requests, commands and instructions: telling someone what to do.

du → Komm!·ihr → Geht!·Sie → Kommen Sie!

English has one command form for everybody; German picks the form by listener — polite Sie stays inside the command.

Kommen Sie bitte mit! · Seien Sie ruhig!
Please come along! · Be quiet!
Komm! — come (one familiar person); Geht! — go (several); Kommen Sie! — come (polite).

sein is irregular here, just like English be.

Sie warten. → Warten Sie bitte!
You are waiting (statement). → Please wait! (request). In the imperative form, the verb comes before Sie.

The verb bracket

Part of the verb often lives at the end.

Verb bracket

Modal verbs

A1forms
Heutemussich in den Ladengehen

Today I have to go to the shop. heute — today; gehen — go; in den Laden — to the shop.

The bracket: muss opens it, the infinitive closes it — English keeps “must … go” together; German lets the two halves drift apart.

  • können → kann — can / be able to
  • müssen → muss — must / have to
  • wollen → will — want to
  • sollen → soll — be supposed to / should
  • dürfen → darf — may / be allowed to
  • mögen → mag — like

Perfekt

A1forms
Ichhabedas schongemacht

Ich habe das schon gemacht — I’ve already done that. machen → gemacht — do → done; habe is the Perfekt auxiliary.

Ich bin nach Hause gegangen.
I went home / have gone home (context decides). gehen → gegangen — go → gone; here directed motion to a destination takes sein.

Separable verbs

A2forms
Ichsteheum 7 Uhrauf

Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf — I get up at 7. aufstehen — get up.

The prefix detaches and flies to the end of the bracket — English phrasal verbs keep their particle beside the verb (“I get up at 7”).

weil ich um 7 Uhr aufstehe
…because I get up at 7. aufstehen — get up; stehe … auf is the separated form.

The passive (Passiv)

B1forms
Das Hauswird2026gebaut

Das Haus wird 2026 gebaut — The house is being built in 2026. Haus — house; bauen → gebaut — build → built; wird forms the passive.

werden + Partizip II — English be swaps for werden.

Konjunktiv II

B1forms

würde + Infinitiv — German would + infinitive; frequent direct forms: wäre, hätte, könnte, möchte, müsste, sollte.

wäre / hätte — would be / would have könnte / möchte — could / would like müsste / sollte — would have to / should
Wenn ich Zeit hätte, würde ich reisen.
Könnten Sie mir helfen?
If I had time, I would travel.
Could you help me? (a polite request).

Spelling: würde (“would”) is not wurde (“became”).

Perfekt with a modal: two infinitives

B1position

Instead of Partizip II — Infinitiv + Infinitiv.

Ich habe arbeiten müssen. Not “gemusst”.
I had to work. müssen — have to; arbeiten — work.

Recognizing it is enough — producing it comes later.

Präteritum: war, hatte…

A2forms

A second way to talk about the past. Spoken German often prefers Perfekt; written narratives often use Präteritum. sein, haben and modals frequently use Präteritum in speech too.

wollte / konnte — wanted to / could musste / sollte / durfte — had to / was supposed to / was allowed to
Ich war krank. · Ich hatte keine Zeit.
I was ill. · I had no time.
sein → war — be → was; haben → hatte — have → had.

German Perfekt can correspond to English simple past or present perfect: Ich habe gegessen — I ate / I have eaten, depending on context.

Subordinate clauses

Conjunction first, conjugated verb to the end.

Nebensätze

weil — because

A1linking
weilich heute arbeitenmuss

weil ich heute arbeiten muss — because I have to work today.

Ich komme nicht, weil ich arbeiten muss.
I’m not coming because I have to work. kommen — come; müssen — have to; arbeiten — work.

English keeps its order after “because” — German sends the verb to the end: weil ich muss arbeiten is the signature error.

dass — that

A2linking
glauben / denken — believe / think hoffen / sagen / wissen — hope / say / know
Ich glaube, dass er heute kommt.
I believe he’s coming today. glauben — believe; kommt — is coming/comes.

Typical after glauben, denken, hoffen, sagen, wissen (believe, think, hope, say, know) — and unlike English “that”, dass is never dropped.

wenn — if / when

A2linking
Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme ich.
If I have time, I’ll come. Zeit — time; habe — have; komme — come.

If the wenn-clause comes first, the main verb follows the comma — not the subject: …, komme ich.

obwohl — although / though

A2linking
Er geht spazieren, obwohl es regnet.
He’s going for a walk although it’s raining. spazieren gehen — go for a walk; es regnet — it’s raining.

ob — if / whether

A2linking
Ich weiß nicht, ob er kommt.
I don’t know whether he’s coming (an embedded yes/no question). wissen → weiß — know (ich form).

A condition “if” → wenn: …, wenn es regnet.

Don’t send the verb to the end

A1linking
und — and aber — but (contrast) denn — because oder — or sondern — but (not A, but B)
Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde.
I’m staying at home because I’m tired. denn — because; normal V2 follows.
Sie kommt nicht aus Bukarest, sondern aus Deva.
She’s not from Bucharest but from Deva. sondern — “but” introducing a correction of a negated alternative.

um … zu / damit — in order to / so that

A2linking
Ich lerne Deutsch, um in Berlin zu arbeiten.
Ich erkläre es langsam, damit du alles verstehst.
I’m learning German in order to work in Berlin (same subject).
I explain it slowly so that you understand everything (different subjects).

Relative clauses

B1linking
Das ist der Mann, der hier arbeitet.
Das ist der Mann, den ich kenne.
That’s the man who works here (der — subject).
That’s the man whom I know (den — object).

Genitiv: der Mann, dessen Auto… (“whose”; fem./plural — deren)

English may drop the pronoun (the man I know) — German never does, and the clause’s verb goes last.

Linking adverbs

B1linking

Not conjunctions: they take position 1, then the verb (see V2):

deshalb — so / therefore trotzdem — nevertheless / even so allerdings — though / however außerdem — besides währenddessen — meanwhile
Es regnete. Deshalb blieben wir zu Hause. · Trotzdem ging er spazieren.
It was raining. So we stayed at home. · Nevertheless, he went for a walk.

English keeps subject + verb after “therefore”; German inverts: Deshalb blieben wir zu Hause — So we stayed at home. The verb comes before wir.

Endings and changes

Small rules that often break an otherwise correct form.

Endungen

Verb endings

A1forms

Stem + ending: -e, -st, -t (ich/du/er). German conjugates every verb the way English only conjugates be — the table is new, the idea isn’t.

English “you work” ends like “I work” — that’s the trap: not du arbeite but du arbeitest.

spielen — play sein — be haben — have

Dativ plural: the noun changes too

A2forms

English nouns have no Dativ ending; German plural nouns normally add -n after den, unless the plural already ends in -n/-s.

mit den Kindern · mit meinen Freunden
With the children · with my friends. Kind → Kinder — child → children; Freund → Freunde — friend → friends.

Plural: five patterns

A1forms

Learn the plural with each noun: -e, -er, -(e)n, -s or no ending; sometimes with an umlaut. Gender is a clue, not a reliable prediction.

die Frau → die Frauen · der Stuhl → die Stühle
das Kind → die Kinder · das Auto → die Autos
Woman → women · chair → chairs.
Child → children · car → cars.

English comparison: Kind → Kinder adds an ending (child → children); Buch → Bücher also changes the vowel (book → books). Loanwords often take -s: Auto → Autos (car → cars).

Genitiv: -s / -es

A2forms

Maskulin/neutrum nouns often take -s/-es — the English ’s in German clothing: des Vaters = the father’s.

das Auto des Vaters
die Farbe des Hauses
The father’s car · the colour of the house. Vater — father; Haus — house.

N-Deklination (weak nouns)

B1forms

A group of masculine nouns takes -n/-en in every case except Nominativ singular — nothing in English prepares you for it: only der Student stays short, everywhere else it’s den/dem/des Studenten.

der Student → den/dem/des Studenten
The student: der Student (subject) → den Studenten (Akk.), dem Studenten (Dat.), des Studenten (Gen.).

Adjectives: don’t learn them blind

A2forms

English adjectives never change — German endings are pure new territory. The shortcut: after der-words the article already shows gender and case, so the adjective mostly just echoes -e/-en; after ein-words it carries more (see the dialog).

der gute Mann · mit dem guten Mann
The good man · with the good man. gut — good; -e/-en are adjective endings.

Comparison: -er / am -sten

A2forms
schnell→schneller→am schnellsten
gut → besser → am besten — good → better → best viel → mehr → am meisten — much → more → most gern → lieber → am liebsten — gladly → preferably → most of all
Heute ist es wärmer. · Ich komme später als du.
It’s warmer today. · I’ll come later than you.
schnell → schneller → am schnellsten — fast → faster → fastest.

The irregulars are English’s own: gut – besser – am besten = good–better–the best · viel – mehr – am meisten = much–more–the most · gern – lieber = to like better.

am = “at the”: am schnellsten is literally “at the fastest” — English says just “the fastest”.
