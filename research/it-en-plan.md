## 1. Recommended product boundary

English-speaking A1–B1 learners need a mobile grammar lookup reference, not a
course or an exam-preparation syllabus. Assume basic grammatical terminology
only when an English explanation accompanies it. Recommend contemporary
standard Italian, with formal/informal and relevant regional labels. English
owns interface copy, learner questions, explanations, glosses and search intent;
Italian terms remain visible alongside understandable English labels.

Six primary retrieval questions:

1. Which article, ending or agreement do I need?
2. Which verb form expresses this situation, especially in the past?
3. Which pronoun replaces this phrase, and where does it go?
4. Which preposition or infinitive pattern expresses this relationship?
5. How do I ask, negate, request or give an instruction?
6. How do I link these ideas or describe a condition?

Recommendations prioritize English interference: grammatical gender rather
than natural gender; agreement with the possessed noun rather than its owner;
subject omission; absence of English do-support; clitics before finite verbs;
piacere's argument structure; Italian aspect rather than English tense-name
substitution; and present/future choices rather than literal translations of
“will” or “going to”. These are pedagogical priorities, not claims that every
English speaker makes the same errors.

The original 30 units were a sketch. The inventory below retains those numbers
and adds IT-31–53 to separate decisions that otherwise disappear into broad
headings. It proposes **53 Required topic units**, including two recognition
units, plus explicit Deferred/Excluded boundaries. That is a research inventory,
not an approved 53-card release commitment. Resolve release size before authoring;
do not obtain “30 cards” by silently dropping mapped syllabus items.

### 3.3 What the disagreements mean

**Observed source allocations:** C puts future, combinations and pluperfect at
B2, present/imperfect subjunctive at B2 and past subjunctive at C1. U includes
these areas earlier, including past subjunctive. R independently labels its
subjunctive introduction B1. These are different curricular choices, not
contradictory grammar facts. [C, U, R]

**Recommended resolution:** keep useful B1 retrieval patterns, explicitly cap
their production depth, and avoid promising CILS certification or exhaustive
B1 coverage. In detail:

- **Subjunctive:** IT-20 should teach a present-reference selection decision
  and a compact present paradigm, frequent irregulars and same-subject
  infinitive alternatives. Keep completed-event recognition separate (IT-51).
  Full sequencing and productive past/irrealis systems remain deferred.
  Grammar fact: construction and register matter; “che always takes subjunctive”
  and “subjunctive means unreal” are false shortcuts. [G5]
- **Future:** include productive future simple (IT-17); compare it with a
  future-reference present. Mention inferential future with an English meaning
  clue, without a large modal taxonomy. Grammar fact: a distant event does not
  mechanically require future; Italian also permits future after se. [G3, G4]
- **Pronoun combinations:** include common indirect + direct/ne order,
  me/te/se/ce/ve and glie- spelling (IT-40), with ordinary finite/infinitive
  placement and a command cross-link. Defer the complete multi-clitic system.
  Grammar fact: glielo can refer to a female recipient; its ending identifies
  the replaced direct object, not the recipient. [G6, G7]
- **Past:** make aspect contrast and auxiliary selection first-class units;
  add a bounded pluperfect to express anteriority. Include a small remoto
  recognition note (IT-52), while deferring its productive paradigm. Grammar
  facts: duration alone does not decide imperfetto versus compound past; remoto
  has regional contemporary use, beyond literature. [G2, G8, G18]

These recommendations still need the scope decisions in §7. Their proposed level
is a supported teaching choice, not a discovered universal CEFR boundary.

## 4. Proposed inventory and surface ownership

`P` = bounded productive retrieval plus recognition; `R` = recognize meaning and
identify the construction, without productive mastery. All IT-01–53 rows below
recommend **Required**. Level ranges distinguish introduction from consolidation;
`B1 supplement` means U/R or a bounded recommendation beyond C's earlier lists.
B1 recognition placement for IT-52 is a proposal; neither baseline explicitly
requires it at B1.

Four proposed views: `forms`, `verbs`, `sentences`, `connections`. Each row owns
one card and one dialog except IT-51/52, which own labeled blocks inside the
named host dialog. In the surface column, `section/card` proposes the actual
anchor `<section>-<card>`; `D:key` proposes a DETAILS key. No anchors are reserved
until acceptance. Tables, where needed, belong to that unit's card/dialog and
carry the topic ID during review. Keep paradigms to a readable six-person table;
no conjugation warehouse.

Every standalone card must show a decision trigger, essential form, glossed
example and any caveat required to avoid a false rule. Each dialog is bounded
to its stated contrast, normally two contrast pairs plus essential exceptions.
Do not hide a correctness caveat behind a chip. English meanings attach as
`gloss` on cards and adjacent `comment` in dialogs; fragments need context too.
Auxiliaries are explained by function, not literal “have/be” translations.
