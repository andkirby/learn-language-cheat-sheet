## 2. Product, learner and language policy

A selective A1–B1 grammar lookup for English-speaking learners, with a one-to-three
tap answer. Contemporary standard Italian; identify relevant regional and
formal/informal differences. This is neither an exhaustive syllabus nor an exam
preparation course. UI, explanations, learner questions and meaning support are
English. Italian terms remain Italian alongside English labels.

Six primary retrieval questions:

1. Which article, ending or agreement do I need?
2. Which verb form expresses this situation, especially in the past?
3. Which pronoun replaces this phrase, and where does it go?
4. Which preposition or infinitive pattern expresses this relationship?
5. How do I ask, negate, request or give an instruction?
6. How do I link these ideas or describe a condition?

Recommendations prioritize English interference: grammatical gender rather
than natural gender; agreement with the possessed noun rather than its owner;
subject omission; absence of English do-support; clitics before finite verbs;
piacere's argument structure; Italian aspect rather than English tense-name
substitution; and present/future choices rather than literal translations of
“will” or “going to”. These are pedagogical priorities, not claims that every
English speaker makes the same errors.

## 4. Information architecture and stable anchors

Four views: `forms` (Forms), `verbs` (Verbs), `sentences` (Sentences),
`connections` (Links). Sections and cards are listed in §7 as `section/card`;
the stable URL anchor is `<section>-<card>`. `D:key` names the owned dialog.
IT-51 is a labeled block in `present-subjunctive`; IT-52 is a labeled block in
`past-choice`. Their host cards visibly advertise the recognition notes.
`patterns` is the verb-pattern section; `infinitives` is the connection-pattern
section, avoiding a global section-ID collision in the proposal.

Do not rename published anchors. No cross-language legacy anchors are invented.
Navigation is lookup-oriented, without an on-page practice destination.

## 5. Content-unit contract

Cards give the trigger, usable forms or a compact decision inventory, a natural
glossed example and a visible limit. Dialogs add contrasts and essential bounded
exceptions. Six-person paradigms use six readable rows; use existing legend or
runs blocks so Italian forms can carry explicit bold markup. Shared table cells
do not support rich runs, so do not inject HTML into them to obtain language tags.

Every Italian fragment requiring study-language treatment uses explicit bold/hit
runs, including inventories and formulas. Every sentence/formula has adjacent
English meaning support; taught words and opaque forms carry contextual clues.
Endings need no separate translation. Explain auxiliaries by construction.
Search uses visible English intent and Italian forms; never index an unseen
dialog topic as though it were a card. Review requires all actual units, examples,
form inventories and translations, including the two hosted recognition blocks.

Shared UI contract: [design system](../../.design/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md).
Review contract: [CONTENT_VALIDATION](../../docs/CONTENT_VALIDATION.md).
