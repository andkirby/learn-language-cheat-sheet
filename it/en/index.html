Nouns

Gender and plural endings.

Noun gender · genere

A1Forms and use

Learn each noun with an article: grammatical gender is not the same as sex.

  • il libro — the book: masculine, often -o
  • la casa — the house: feminine, often -a
  • il pane / la notte — bread / night: -e can be either gender
La notte è lunga.
The night is long. notte is feminine, so lunga has -a.
Endings are clues, not guarantees. Check the noun with its article.

Noun plurals · plurale

A1Forms and use

Change the noun ending and the article together.

  • libro → libri — book → books; normally -o → -i
  • casa → case — house → houses; feminine -a → -e
  • notte → notti — night → nights; -e → -i
  • problema → problemi — problem → problems; masculine -a → -i
Le case sono piccole.
The houses are small.
Not every noun changes: learn irregular and invariant plurals with the noun.

Articles

Choose the form and the intended reference.

The · definite articles · il / lo / la

A1Forms and use

Match gender and number, then the sound of the next word.

  • il → i — masculine, most consonants: the book(s)
  • lo → gli — masculine, s + consonant, z, gn, ps: the student(s)
  • l’ → gli — masculine before a vowel: the friend(s)
  • la / l’ → le — feminine: the house(s), the female friend(s)
Lo studente legge il libro.
The student reads the book.
The next word may be an adjective. Article use does not match English in every context.

A/an · un / uno / una / un’

A1Forms and use

Use an indefinite article for one nonspecific person or thing.

  • un libro / un amico — a book / a male friend
  • uno studente / uno zaino — a student / a backpack
  • una casa / un’amica — a house / a female friend
Ho un amico e un’amica a Roma.
I have a male friend and a female friend in Rome.
Masculine un has no apostrophe before a vowel. These articles have no plural form.

Some · partitive articles · del / dei

A2Forms and use

For an unspecified amount or some countable items, di + article can act as a partitive.

  • del pane / della pasta — some bread / some pasta
  • dell’acqua — some water
  • dei libri / degli amici / delle mele — some books / friends / apples
Compro del pane e delle mele.
I’m buying some bread and some apples.
Partitives are not compulsory everywhere. The same forms can mean of/from the.

Determiners

Possession, pointing and quantities.

My/his/her · possessivi

A1Forms and use

The possessive agrees with what is possessed, not with the owner’s gender.

  • il mio / la mia / i miei / le mie — my: masculine/feminine, singular/plural
  • il tuo / il suo — your (informal singular) / his or her: same ending pattern
  • nostro / vostro / loro — our / your (plural) / their; the last form is invariant
La sua macchina è nuova.
His or her car is new. sua agrees with macchina, not the owner.
Normally use article + possessive. Singular family nouns have a bounded exception.

This/that · questo / quello

A1Forms and use

Point to something near or further away; match the noun’s gender and number.

  • questo / questa / questi / queste — this / these: masculine/feminine
  • quel libro / quei libri — that book / those books
  • quello studente / quegli studenti — that student / those students
  • quell’amico / quegli amici — that friend / those friends
Questo libro è mio; quello è tuo.
This book is mine; that one is yours.
Before a masculine noun, quello changes like an article. Alone, use quello/quelli, not quel/quei.

All, some, each, none · indefiniti

B1Forms and use

Decide whether the word counts things, distributes them, or replaces the noun.

  • molto / tanto / poco — much/many / so much/many / little/few; agree before a noun
  • tutto il / tutti i — all the: match the noun
  • ogni / qualche + singular noun — each/every / some/a few
  • nessuno / qualcuno / qualcosa — nobody / somebody / something
Leggo qualche libro ogni mese.
I read a few books every month. libro and mese stay singular here.
Qualche and ogni take singular nouns. English “a few” does not justify a plural after qualche.

Adjectives

Agreement, position and comparison.

Beautiful · bel / bello / bei / begli

B1Forms and use

Before a noun, masculine bello follows article-like sound choices.

  • bel libro / bei libri — beautiful book / books
  • bello spettacolo / begli spettacoli — beautiful show / shows
  • bell’albero / begli alberi — beautiful tree / trees
  • bella casa / belle case — beautiful house / houses
Ho un bel libro e dei bei quaderni.
I have a beautiful book and some beautiful notebooks.
After the noun or essere, use ordinary bello/bella/belli/belle. Most adjectives do not shorten this way.

Adjective agreement · accordo

A2Forms and use

An adjective describes a noun and follows its gender and number.

  • nuovo / nuova / nuovi / nuove — new: four forms
  • grande → grandi — big: one singular, one plural form for both genders
  • case nuove / libri nuovi — new houses / new books
Le chiavi sono piccole.
The keys are small. chiave is feminine; chiavi is plural.
Agreement still applies after essere. Some adjectives are invariant.

Adjective position · before or after?

B1Forms and use

A descriptive adjective often follows the noun; some common adjectives can precede it.

  • una casa rossa — a red house: classifying colour after noun
  • un film italiano — an Italian film: relation/origin after noun
  • un vecchio amico — a long-standing friend
  • un amico vecchio — an elderly friend
Ho incontrato un vecchio amico.
I met a long-standing friend.
Position can change emphasis or meaning. “All Italian adjectives follow the noun” is false.

Comparisons · più / meno / -issimo

B1Forms and use

Compare a property, or distinguish “the most” from “very”.

  • più alto di / meno alto di — taller than / less tall than
  • alto come / tanto alto quanto — as tall as
  • il più alto / altissimo — the tallest / very tall
  • migliore / peggiore — better / worse: irregular comparisons
Luca è più alto di Marco.
Luca is taller than Marco.
-issimo means very/extremely, not “the most in a group”. The comparison link is not always di.

Adverbs

Form and position.

Adverbs in -mente

B1Forms and use

Many manner adverbs use an adjective’s feminine singular base + -mente.

  • lenta → lentamente — slow → slowly
  • felice → felicemente — happy → happily
  • normale → normalmente — normal → normally
  • regolare → regolarmente — regular → regularly
Parla lentamente, per favore.
Speak slowly, please.
Before -mente, adjectives in -le/-re often drop final e. Not every English -ly word uses -mente.

Adverbs: well, already, never, here

A1Forms and use

Adverbs describe a verb, adjective or another adverb; place depends on the word and focus.

  • bene / male — well / badly: how
  • già / sempre / mai — already / always / never or ever
  • qui / lì / prima / poi — here / there / before / then
  • molto / poco / più / meno — a lot / little / more / less
Non ho mai visto quel film.
I have never seen that film.
No single position rule covers all adverbs. mai can mean “ever” in a question.

Numbers and dates

Closed patterns for amounts, dates and times.

Numbers, dates and clock time

A2Forms and use

Use cardinal numbers for quantities; ordinals agree with nouns.

  • uno, due, tre, quattro, cinque — one, two, three, four, five
  • sei, sette, otto, nove, dieci — six, seven, eight, nine, ten
  • primo / secondo / terzo — first / second / third; change endings
  • È l’una / Sono le due — It is one o’clock / two o’clock
La lezione è il primo maggio alle due.
The lesson is on the first of May at two o’clock.
Dates normally use cardinals after day one. Ordinals change for gender and number.

Present forms

Regular patterns and essential irregular verbs.

Present tense · presente

A1Forms and use

The present can describe a habit, a current situation or an action happening now.

  • parlare → parlo, parli, parla; parliamo, parlate, parlano — speak; I, you, he/she, we, you plural, they
  • credere → credo, credi, crede; crediamo, credete, credono — believe; same person order
  • dormire → dormo, dormi, dorme; dormiamo, dormite, dormono — sleep; same person order
  • finire → finisco, finisci, finisce; finiamo, finite, finiscono — finish; -isc- in four persons
Parlo italiano e lavoro qui.
I speak Italian and work here.
Not every -ire verb uses -isc-. Italian present can translate English “am doing” without a progressive.

Essential irregulars · essere / avere

A1Forms and use

Keep frequent irregular forms together; the Italian grammatical subject controls the verb ending, even when its pronoun is omitted.

  • essere → sono / sei / è / siamo / siete / sono — be; I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they
  • avere → ho / hai / ha / abbiamo / avete / hanno — have; same person order
  • vado / faccio / vengo / so — I go / I do-make / I come / I know how or know a fact
Ho una chiave e sono a casa.
I have a key and I am at home.
An io form does not predict the whole paradigm. Modals have their own forms in the modals card.

Verb patterns

Modals, reflexives and liking.

Reflexive and reciprocal · mi / ti / si

B1Forms and use

Match the pronoun to the subject; many routine verbs have a pronominal form.

  • mi lavo / ti lavi / si lava — I wash myself / you wash yourself / he-she washes
  • ci laviamo / vi lavate / si lavano — we wash / you plural wash / they wash
  • alzarsi / incontrarsi — get up / meet each other: base forms end in -si
Ci siamo incontrati ieri.
We met each other yesterday.
Not every -si verb means literally “oneself”. Personal reflexive/reciprocal compound forms use essere.

Can, must, want · potere / dovere / volere

A1Forms and use

Put the modal before an infinitive, without adding a or di.

  • posso, puoi, può; possiamo, potete, possono — can: I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they
  • devo, devi, deve; dobbiamo, dovete, devono — must/have to: same order
  • voglio, vuoi, vuole; vogliamo, volete, vogliono — want: same order
Non posso venire, ma voglio aiutarti.
I can’t come, but I want to help you.
Do not insert the English word “to”. sapere + infinitive often expresses a learned skill.

I like… · mi piace / mi piacciono

A2Forms and use

The liked thing is the grammatical subject; the person experiencing the liking is indirect.

  • mi / ti / gli / le — to me / to you / to him / to her
  • piace + singular / infinitive — is pleasing: I like one thing or an activity
  • piacciono + plural — are pleasing: I like several things
Mi piacciono questi libri.
I like these books. libri controls the plural verb.
Do not make the experiencer an English-style subject of piace.

Past time

Forms, auxiliaries and viewpoints.

Completed past · passato prossimo

A1Forms and use

Use a present auxiliary + past participle for a past event viewed as a whole.

  • ho parlato / ho creduto / ho dormito — I spoke / believed / slept: -ato/-uto/-ito
  • sono arrivato / sono arrivata — I arrived: masculine / feminine
  • ho fatto / ho detto / ho visto — I did-made / said / saw: irregular participles
Ieri ho lavorato a casa.
Yesterday I worked at home.
This tense often translates English simple past. It is not limited to recent events or English “have done”.

Auxiliary choice · essere / avere

A2Forms and use

Learn the auxiliary with the verb and construction; meaning and pronoun position can matter.

  • ho letto / ho camminato — I read / walked: have auxiliary
  • sono partito / sono partita — I left: be auxiliary + subject agreement
  • mi sono alzato / alzata — I got up: personal reflexive
  • ho finito / è finito — I finished / it finished: changed construction
Ho camminato, poi sono tornata a casa.
I walked, then I returned home (female speaker).
“All motion verbs take essere” is false. Some verbs and modals allow alternatives.

Past-participle agreement · accordo

B1Forms and use

Check the auxiliary and the function of any preceding pronoun.

  • Anna è arrivata / i ragazzi sono arrivati — be auxiliary: subject controls ending
  • ho comprato le mele — have auxiliary, following object: normally -o
  • le ho comprate — preceding feminine them: agree with object
  • ne ho comprate due — I bought two of them: feminine items
Le chiavi? Le ho trovate.
The keys? I found them. le replaces feminine plural chiavi.
Not every preceding object behaves like lo/la/li/le. Nonpartitive ne does not trigger this object agreement.

Past background · imperfetto

A2Forms and use

Use the imperfect to view a past situation from inside: background, habit or an action underway.

  • parlavo, parlavi, parlava; parlavamo, parlavate, parlavano — parlare = speak: I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they
  • credevo / dormivo — I believed / I slept or was sleeping
  • ero, eri, era; eravamo, eravate, erano — essere = be: same order
Da piccolo giocavo qui.
As a child I used to play here (male speaker).
The imperfect is a viewpoint, not simply “a long action” or every English “was”.

Past choice · event or background?

A2Forms and use

Choose the viewpoint in context: a bounded event, or a situation viewed from inside.

  • ho letto il libro — I read the book: whole event
  • leggevo quando… — I was reading when…: background
  • andavo ogni estate — I used to go every summer: habit
Leggevo quando mi hai chiamato.
I was reading when you called me.
Duration and repetition alone do not decide the tense. Recognition: passato remoto forms are explained in Details.

Earlier than a past moment · trapassato prossimo

B1Forms and use

Use an imperfect auxiliary + participle to place an event before another past reference point.

  • avevo / avevi / aveva + participle — I / you / he-she had done
  • avevamo / avevate / avevano + participle — we / you plural / they had done
  • ero / eri / era + participle — I / you / he-she had arrived, etc. with the be auxiliary
Quando sono arrivata, il treno era già partito.
When I arrived, the train had already left (female speaker).
Choose the auxiliary as for the compound past. già is useful context, not a compulsory trigger.

Future reference

Plans, predictions and inference.

Future reference · futuro semplice

B1Forms and use

Use a future form for a prediction or later event; a present can also express a contextualized plan.

  • parlerò, parlerai, parlerà; parleremo, parlerete, parleranno — I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they will speak
  • crederò / dormirò — I will believe / sleep: same future endings
  • sarò / avrò / andrò / farò — I will be / have / go / do
Domani parto; tornerò lunedì.
I’m leaving tomorrow; I’ll return on Monday.
Distance alone does not force a tense. Future can also express a present-time guess.

Requests and commands

Conditional, tu/voi and formal Lei.

Polite requests and advice · condizionale

B1Forms and use

Use present conditional for a softened request, desire, advice or uncertainty.

  • parlerei, parleresti, parlerebbe; parleremmo, parlereste, parlerebbero — I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they would speak
  • vorrei / potresti / dovrebbe — I would like / could you / he-she should
  • sarei / avrei — I would be / have
Potresti aiutarmi? Vorrei capire.
Could you help me? I’d like to understand.
English “would” has several uses. This card does not teach the full unreal-condition system.

Commands to tu/voi · imperativo

A1Forms and use

Choose informal singular tu or plural voi; negative tu uses the infinitive.

  • parla / parlate — speak: informal singular / plural
  • prendi / prendete — take: informal singular / plural
  • dormi / dormite; finisci / finite — sleep; finish: informal singular / plural
  • non parlare / non parlate — don’t speak: informal singular / plural
Prendi il libro e leggilo.
Take the book and read it (tu).
Positive tu/voi commands attach object pronouns. Formal Lei uses a different pattern.

Polite instructions to Lei

B1Forms and use

Formal singular address uses third-person present-subjunctive forms as instructions.

  • parli / prenda / dorma — speak / take / sleep: formal singular
  • scusi / dica / venga — excuse me / say-tell / come: formal singular
  • non entri — do not enter: formal singular
  • mi dica / lo prenda — tell me / take it: pronoun before formal command
Mi dica, per favore.
Please tell me (formal singular address).
Unlike positive tu commands, formal Lei commands place the pronoun before the verb.

Subjunctive

Bounded B1 patterns, with recognition notes.

Present subjunctive · congiuntivo presente

B1Forms and use

Certain constructions select a mood: begin with common opinions, wishes and necessity.

  • che io parli / che tu parli / che lui parli — that I / you / he speak: -are singular uses -i
  • che noi parliamo / voi parliate / loro parlino — that we / you plural / they speak
  • che io creda / dorma / finisca — that I believe / sleep / finish
Spero che tu stia bene.
I hope you are well.
Not every che clause takes subjunctive. Recognition: congiuntivo passato (past subjunctive) is included in Details; full sequencing is deferred.

Ongoing and imminent

Two different stare constructions.

Ongoing action · stare + gerundio

B1Forms and use

Use this construction to focus on a process underway at a particular moment.

  • sto / stai / sta / stiamo / state / stanno — present of stay/be: I / you / he-she / we / you plural / they
  • parlando / credendo / dormendo — speaking / believing / sleeping: -ando or -endo
  • stavo leggendo — I was reading: imperfect of stay/be + gerund
Sto leggendo; ti chiamo dopo.
I’m reading; I’ll call you later.
The simple present can also describe what is happening now. Do not use the progressive for every English -ing.

About to · stare per + infinitive

B1Forms and use

Use stare per when an event is on the point of happening.

  • sto per partire — I am about to leave
  • sta per finire — it is about to end
  • stavamo per uscire — we were about to go out
Sto per uscire. Hai le chiavi?
I’m about to go out. Do you have the keys?
This means imminent, not every intention expressed by English “going to”.

Sentence basics

Subjects, questions, negatives and generic statements.

Subjects and address · io / tu / Lei

A1Forms and use

The ending identifies the subject when context is clear; say the pronoun for emphasis or clarification.

  • io / tu / lui / lei — I / you informal / he / she
  • noi / voi / loro — we / you plural / they
  • Lei parla / tu parli — you speak: formal singular / informal singular
Parlo inglese. E tu?
I speak English. And you?
Italian often omits a recoverable subject. Formal Lei still takes third-person singular verb forms.

Questions · chi / cosa / quale / quanto

A1Forms and use

A yes/no question can keep statement word order. Question words identify what you need to know.

  • chi / che cosa / cosa — who / what
  • dove / quando / come / perché — where / when / how / why
  • quale / quali — which: singular / plural
  • quanto / quanta / quanti / quante — how much/many: agree with a noun
Quanti libri hai?
How many books do you have?
No English do-support or obligatory subject–verb inversion. Intonation distinguishes many spoken yes/no questions.

Not, never, nobody · non / mai / nessuno

A1Forms and use

Put non before the finite verb and any object pronouns.

  • non capisco / non lo capisco — I don’t understand / I don’t understand it
  • non…mai / non…più — never / no longer
  • nessuno / niente — nobody / nothing
  • sì / no — yes / no: standalone answers
Non ho mai visto nessuno qui.
I have never seen anyone here.
Negative words after the verb normally need non in negative statements. Before the verb, nessuno/niente can negate without non.

What a…! How…! · che / quanto

A2Forms and use

Use a short exclamatory pattern to highlight a quality or amount.

  • Che bello! / Che bella casa! — How lovely! / What a beautiful house!
  • Che fortuna! — What luck!
  • Quanti libri! / Quanta gente! — So many books! / So many people!
Che bella giornata!
What a beautiful day!
Che stays unchanged. Quanto changes with a following noun; punctuation and context distinguish an exclamation from a question.

Generic people · si impersonale

B1Forms and use

Use si for an unspecified human agent: people, generic you, or sometimes we.

  • si parte / si dorme — people leave / sleep: impersonal singular
  • si mangia bene — people eat well: verb used without an object
  • si vendono libri — books are sold: passive construction, plural
  • ci si lava — one washes oneself: reflexive + impersonal
In Italia si parla italiano.
People speak Italian in Italy.
Not every si means “oneself”, and not every si construction has a singular verb.

Object pronouns

Function before position.

Direct objects · lo / la / li / le

A2Forms and use

Replace a direct object without adding a preposition; match its gender and number.

  • mi / ti / ci / vi — me / you / us / you plural
  • lo / la / li / le — him-it / her-it / them masculine / them feminine
  • lo vedo / voglio vederlo — I see it / I want to see it
Le chiavi? Le cerco.
The keys? I’m looking for them. le replaces feminine plural chiavi.
A normal finite verb has the clitic before it. Infinitives and positive informal commands use attachment.

Indirect objects · gli / le

B1Forms and use

Replace a recipient or experiencer introduced by a in the verb’s pattern.

  • mi / ti / ci / vi — to me / to you / to us / to you plural
  • gli / le — to him / to her; singular
  • a loro / gli — to them; explicit phrase / common modern clitic
Le do il libro.
I give her the book. le means to her here, not feminine plural them.
The same spelling can serve a different function: identify the verb’s recipient versus direct object.

Two pronouns · me lo / glielo

B1Forms and use

In common recipient + direct-object combinations, put the recipient first.

  • me / te / se / ce / ve + lo/la/li/le/ne — recipient/reflexive form changes before the second clitic
  • glielo / gliela / glieli / gliele / gliene — to him or her + it/them/of it: joined spelling
  • me lo dà / vuole darmelo — he-she gives it to me / wants to give it to me
Il libro? Glielo do domani.
The book? I’ll give it to him/her tomorrow.
The final lo/la/li/le identifies the direct object, not the recipient’s gender. This is a bounded common ordering pattern.

ci and ne

Recover the intended phrase, not an English word.

ci · us, there, or a verb pattern?

B1Forms and use

Recover the phrase ci stands for: a person group, a place or a selected complement.

  • ci vede — he/she sees us: personal object
  • ci vado — I go there: place
  • c’è / ci sono — there is / there are
  • ci penso / ci credo — I think about it / I believe in it
Vai a Roma? Sì, ci vado domani.
Are you going to Rome? Yes, I’m going there tomorrow.
ci is not one fixed English word and does not replace every prepositional phrase.

ne · of it, of them, about it

B1Forms and use

Use ne for a quantity taken from an identified set, or in selected di-phrases.

  • ne voglio due — I want two of them
  • non ne voglio nessuno — I don’t want any of them (masculine items)
  • ne parlo — I talk about it: the pattern talk about
  • ne / né — pronoun / neither-nor conjunction: accent distinguishes
Quante mele vuoi? Ne voglio due.
How many apples do you want? I want two of them.
Keep the quantity when it is needed. ne does not always mean “some”; full idiomatic verb inventories are deferred.

Prepositions

Relations and article contractions.

Simple prepositions · di / a / da / in

A1Forms and use

Choose a relationship in the Italian construction, rather than one English equivalent.

  • di / a / da / in — of-about / to-at / from-at someone’s / in-to
  • con / su / per — with / on-about / for-through
  • tra / fra — between-among or in a future interval: alternatives
Vado da Anna con Luca.
I’m going to Anna’s with Luca.
Each preposition has several uses. A verb’s selected preposition may differ from English.

Preposition + the · preposizioni articolate

A2Forms and use

Combine di/a/da/in/su with the definite article required by the next word.

Article order for the rows: il / lo / l’ / la / i / gli / le.

  • di → del / dello / dell’ / della / dei / degli / delle — of: article order shown above
  • a → al / allo / all’ / alla / ai / agli / alle — to-at: same article order
  • da → dal / dallo / dall’ / dalla / dai / dagli / dalle — from-at: same order
  • in → nel / nello / nell’ / nella / nei / negli / nelle — in-to: same order
  • su → sul / sullo / sull’ / sulla / sui / sugli / sulle — on-about: same order
Il libro è nello zaino, vicino alla porta.
The book is in the backpack, near the door.
Do not fuse every preposition. con usually stays separate; col/coi also occur. per/tra/fra normally stay separate.

Time and place

Destinations, duration and time intervals.

Place and duration · a / in / da / per

A2Forms and use

Choose the location or time relationship, then the construction’s tense.

  • a Roma / in Italia / da Anna — in-to Rome / in-to Italy / at-to Anna’s
  • da due anni — for two years, continuing from the past
  • per due ore / due ore fa — for two hours (duration) / two hours ago
  • tra due giorni / alle due — in two days / at two o’clock
Vivo qui da due anni.
I’ve lived here for two years. The situation is still continuing.
Ongoing duration commonly uses Italian present + da, even when English uses a perfect tense. Place choices have lexical exceptions.

Linking clauses

Connections, relatives and real conditions.

Relative clauses · che / cui / dove

B1Forms and use

Link a description to a noun: use che for a subject or direct object, preposition + cui for other relations.

  • il libro che leggo — the book that I’m reading: object
  • la persona che parla — the person who is speaking: subject
  • la persona con cui parlo — the person I’m talking to: prepositional relation
  • la casa dove abito — the house where I live: place
Questo è il libro che cerco.
This is the book I’m looking for.
Do not omit che as you can omit English object “that”. Do not strand the preposition after cui.

Add, contrast, explain, conclude

A2Forms and use

Select the relation between ideas before selecting a connector.

  • e / ma / o — and / but / or
  • invece / infatti — instead-whereas / in fact-for
  • allora / quindi — then-so / therefore
  • non solo…ma anche — not only…but also
Sono stanca, quindi resto a casa.
I’m tired, so I’m staying home (female speaker).
These words do not all introduce the same clause type. allora can also mean “then” in time.

That, when, because, in order to

B1Forms and use

Choose a finite clause with a subject, or a bounded infinitive pattern.

  • so che… / penso di… — I know that… / I think I will… (same subject)
  • quando / mentre / prima di — when / while / before + infinitive
  • perché / per + infinitive — because / in order to
Leggo per capire, perché il testo è difficile.
I read in order to understand, because the text is difficult.
Cause perché and purpose perché need different mood choices. This card does not teach full tense backshift.

Real/open if · se + indicative

B1Forms and use

For a condition treated as open or real, use indicative in the se clause.

  • se piove, resto — if it rains, I stay
  • se piove, resterò — if it rains, I’ll stay
  • se verrà, parleremo — if he-she comes, we’ll talk
  • se hai tempo, chiamami — if you have time, call me
Se hai tempo, vieni con noi.
If you have time, come with us.
Italian permits future after se. Unreal and past-counterfactual systems are deferred; do not extend this pattern to every English “if”.

Verb + infinitive

Learn the linking pattern with the verb.

Verb + infinitive · a / di / no link

B1Forms and use

Learn the first verb with its infinitive pattern; English “to” does not select a or di.

  • posso / devo / voglio + infinitive — can / must / want: no added preposition
  • comincio a / imparo a — I begin to / learn to
  • cerco di / finisco di — I try to / finish doing
  • penso di / spero di — I think I will / hope to: same subject
Cerco di capire e comincio a scrivere.
I try to understand and start writing.
These are selected patterns, not a rule that every verb takes the same link. che + finite verb can introduce a different subject.
